## Installation

<InstallCommand name="drawer" />

The CLI writes `components/ui/drawer.tsx`, `lib/motion.ts` and `lib/overlay.ts`, installs [Direction](/native/components/nativewind/direction), [Text](/native/components/nativewind/text) and Utils, and adds `@rn-primitives/dialog`, `@rn-primitives/portal`,
`react-native-reanimated` and `react-native-worklets`. The content draws in the app's `PortalHost`; see [Add a
portal host](/native/docs/installation#add-a-portal-host). Without one it draws nothing, and logs an error saying
so while the app is in development.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Button } from '@/components/ui/button';
import {
  Drawer,
  DrawerClose,
  DrawerContent,
  DrawerDescription,
  DrawerFooter,
  DrawerHeader,
  DrawerTitle,
  DrawerTrigger,
} from '@/components/ui/drawer';
import { Text } from '@/components/ui/text';
```

```tsx
<Drawer showSwipeHandle>
  <DrawerTrigger asChild>
    <Button variant="outline">
      <Text>Open Drawer</Text>
    </Button>
  </DrawerTrigger>
  <DrawerContent>
    <DrawerHeader>
      <DrawerTitle>Move Goal</DrawerTitle>
      <DrawerDescription>Set your daily activity goal.</DrawerDescription>
    </DrawerHeader>
    <DrawerFooter>
      <DrawerClose asChild>
        <Button variant="outline">
          <Text>Cancel</Text>
        </Button>
      </DrawerClose>
    </DrawerFooter>
  </DrawerContent>
</Drawer>
```

## API reference

### Drawer

Takes every prop of React Native's `View`, plus:

| Prop              | Type                                  | Default  |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------- |
| `swipeDirection`  | `'down' \| 'up' \| 'left' \| 'right'` | `'down'` |
| `showSwipeHandle` | `boolean`                             | `false`  |
| `open`            | `boolean`                             | -        |
| `defaultOpen`     | `boolean`                             | -        |
| `onOpenChange`    | `(open: boolean) => void`             | -        |

`swipeDirection` is the way a swipe closes the drawer, which also attaches it to that edge: `down` to the bottom,
`up` to the top, and `left` or `right` to that side. `showSwipeHandle` draws a bar on the drawer's inner edge.
Without `open` the Drawer holds its own state, starting from `defaultOpen`.

### DrawerTrigger and DrawerClose

Take every prop of React Native's `Pressable`. DrawerTrigger opens the Drawer and DrawerClose closes it. Pass
`asChild` to make a Button do it. Focus returns to the trigger when the Drawer closes.

### DrawerContent

Takes every prop of React Native's `View`, plus `portalHost`, the name of the `PortalHost` to draw in when it is not
the app's own. A bottom or top drawer is as tall as its content, up to 6rem short of the screen, with its header
centred; a side drawer is three quarters of the screen's width. The drawer is a modal `dialog` named by its
DrawerTitle, over a dimmed overlay, and accessibility focus moves into it as it opens.

VoiceOver stays inside the drawer while it is open. TalkBack does once the app hides its screens from screen
readers while an overlay is shown, as [Add a portal host](/native/docs/installation#add-a-portal-host) shows; the
web hides the rest of the page itself.

A swipe toward its edge moves it with the finger. Let go past a quarter of its size, or flicked, it closes;
otherwise it settles back. A press on the overlay, the Android back button, the iOS escape gesture (a two-finger Z)
and Escape on the web close it too. It slides in from its edge over 450ms and out over 400ms. When the system asks
for reduced motion it appears and goes at once; a swipe still moves it.

The drawer runs to the screen's edge, under the home indicator or the status bar. Pad it by the safe area's insets
where it meets them, such as with `useSafeAreaInsets` from `react-native-safe-area-context`.

### DrawerHeader and DrawerFooter

Take every prop of React Native's `View`. The footer is pushed to the end of the drawer and stacks its children.

### DrawerTitle and DrawerDescription

Take every prop of [Text](/native/components/nativewind/text). The title is a heading.

### DrawerSwipeHandle, DrawerOverlay and DrawerPortal

DrawerContent draws a DrawerSwipeHandle when the Drawer has `showSwipeHandle`, and a DrawerOverlay inside a
DrawerPortal, so you only need them to build content of your own. The handle is hidden from screen readers.

## Right to left

A drawer swiped `left` or `right` stays on the edge it names, as upstream's does, which places it with `left-0`
and `right-0`. A device whose text reads right to left draws `left` styles on the right, unless the app turned
that off with `I18nManager.swapLeftAndRightInRTL(false)`; there a swipe toward the edge it is drawn on closes it.

## Differences from upstream

- Upstream's `render` prop becomes `asChild`, as on every native item.
- `snapPoints`, `modal`, nested drawers and their stacking are left out. A drawer here is always modal and opens
  to its full size.
- On iOS, VoiceOver does not read `aria-labelledby`, so the drawer is not announced by its title's name there.
  Focus moves into the content, where the title is the first thing read. Android and the web read the name.
- The close after a swipe runs over 400ms whatever the swipe's speed, where upstream scales it by the swipe's
  strength. The overlay fades with the swipe, as upstream's does.
- On the web, a drag that starts on text selects the text rather than moving the drawer.
- Upstream widens a side drawer to 24rem from `sm` and blurs the screen behind the overlay. A phone is narrower
  than `sm`, and React Native has no backdrop blur.
- Upstream's animation runs whatever the system's motion setting. The native one is skipped when the system asks
  for reduced motion.
- `portalHost` is added, for an app that draws overlays into a `PortalHost` of its own.
